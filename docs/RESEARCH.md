## 1. Financial-disclosure data sources: licensing, leakage, redistribution

### 1.1 SEC EDGAR (primary corpus) — CORRECTED per direct legal-nuance review (2026-10-31)

**Previous draft of this section overstated a blanket "public domain" claim for EDGAR filings. That claim is wrong and has been corrected below; do not rely on any earlier version of this document.**

- **17 U.S.C. §105 applies only to works prepared by U.S. federal government employees as part of their official duties.** It does **not** extend to content merely *hosted* on a government website or system. EDGAR is a government-operated filing/hosting system, but the overwhelming majority of filing content — 10-Ks, 10-Qs, 8-Ks, proxy statements, registration statements, exhibits — is **authored by private parties** (the issuer, its counsel, auditors, or other third parties) and submitted *to* the SEC, not created *by* SEC/government staff.
- **Corrected distinction:**
  - **Government-created content** on sec.gov (SEC staff writings, rulemaking text, SEC-authored forms/instructions, agency guidance) — this narrower category is consistent with §105 public-domain treatment, and SEC's own public FAQ (verified via Wayback Archive mirror dated 2026-09-30; live `sec.gov` fetch returned HTTP 403 this session — treat as reliable-but-indirect) states Government-created content is "free to access and reuse."
  - **Issuer-authored filing content** (the actual 10-K narrative, MD&A prose, footnote text, exhibits, and any third-party-authored material embedded in a filing) is **not** a government work and is **not** automatically public domain merely because it is publicly accessible via EDGAR. Public accessibility ≠ public domain. Secondary legal commentary (e.g., a law-firm client alert discussing EDGAR-document copyright, `pattishall.com/pdf/8-17-10_Copyright_Infringement_in_Edgar_Docs_Blog_Post.pdf` — fetched but PDF text extraction was not fully legible in this session, cited for the general point only, **not as a verified verbatim quote**) and general copyright-law analysis both support that courts have treated unauthorized mass copying/resale of filing content as potentially infringing, and that purely factual data (raw numbers, financial-statement figures) is generally not copyrightable while the surrounding narrative expression can be.
- **Formats** (per SEC docs, referenced but not all independently re-fetched this session): full-text 10-K/10-Q/8-K (`/Archives/edgar/data/...`), XBRL structured facts (`data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json`), EDGAR full-text search API (`sec.gov/edgar/sec-api-documentation`), bulk data (`sec.gov/dera/data`).
- **Corrected design implication:** EDGAR access itself is free and SEC imposes no *access* restriction, but this project must **not** treat issuer-authored filing text as a freely redistributable asset. Practical consequences:
  - Factual/numerical data extracted from filings (XBRL facts, raw financial-statement numbers) is the safest layer to index, cache, and redistribute, consistent with the general rule that facts are not copyrightable.
  - Narrative/footnote text (MD&A, risk factors, explanatory notes) should be treated as **subject to the issuer's copyright**, used under fair-use-like RAG-retrieval norms (retrieve small relevant excerpts at query time for grounding/citation, analogous to search-engine snippet use) rather than bulk-copied/redistributed as a standalone corpus.
  - A reference/demo build should prefer fetching filing text live from EDGAR at indexing time (or from a licensed provider) rather than vendoring large verbatim filing excerpts into this repository.
  - This caveat does **not** change the licensing analysis of the benchmark datasets in §1.2 (those are separately licensed by their respective publishers), but it **does** mean a dataset's own permissive license (e.g., TAT-QA's CC-BY-4.0) licenses that publisher's curation/question/answer layer — it does not, by itself, certify that the underlying issuer-authored filing excerpts embedded in the dataset are free of the issuer's own copyright. Treat dataset-license and source-document-copyright as two separate questions.

### 1.3 Decision for this project
- **Primary corpus:** SEC EDGAR filings fetched directly from EDGAR (free, unrestricted *access*; **not** a blanket public-domain/copyright-free claim — see §1.1 correction). Factual/XBRL data is treated as safely cacheable/redistributable; issuer-authored narrative text is retrieved live and used in small cited excerpts for grounding, not bulk-vendored into the repository as a redistributable corpus.
- **Evaluation:** use the **open 150-sample FinanceBench set** and **TAT-QA** (CC-BY-4.0, fully permissive) as the two default eval sets — both can legally ship inside a reference repo's eval harness (FinanceBench sample with attribution + NC compliance; TAT-QA with attribution only). **FinQA/ConvFinQA/DocFinQA (MIT)** are usable for additional numerical-reasoning regression tests, but note the FinQA license-string discrepancy above in any compliance review. **FinDER** is useful for retrieval-precision stress testing but is NC-licensed and its question/evidence pairs must not be redistributed outside eval-harness use without checking the terms-of-use attribution clause.
- **Explicit non-goal:** do not copy or redistribute raw copyrighted dataset text (e.g., full FinanceBench 10,231-question set, FinDER triplets beyond fair eval use) into this repository. Only the already-open subsets (150-sample FinanceBench, TAT-QA, FinQA/ConvFinQA/DocFinQA) are covered by their publishers' own stated licenses, and even those should be pulled at eval-run time from their official source rather than vendored verbatim, to track upstream corrections. **Corrected caveat:** "covered by a stated license" describes the publishing research team's own grant over their curated question/answer/excerpt layer — it is not an independent guarantee that every embedded filing excerpt is free of the originating issuer's copyright (see §1.1 correction); treat the publisher's license as the operative permission for using the dataset as published, not as a certification about the underlying source documents.
- **Leakage mitigation plan:** (a) always report both retrieval-only and closed-book (no-retrieval) scores for the same LLM to estimate contamination; (b) prefer newer/unique company-quarter combinations not likely in pretraining cutoffs where feasible; (c) treat any eval score as an upper bound, not a guarantee of production generalization.

## 2. Retrieval technique comparison

| Technique | Status / citation | Fit for financial-disclosure RAG |
|---|---|---|
| **Hybrid dense + BM25** | Native in Qdrant (`qdrant.tech/documentation/search/hybrid-search/`), Weaviate (`docs.weaviate.io/.../hybrid-search`), Elasticsearch (`elastic.co/elasticsearch/hybrid-search`) — all verified official docs, current. | **Baseline recommendation.** BM25 wins on exact identifiers (ticker symbols, CIK/accession numbers, GAAP line-item names); dense wins on paraphrased questions. Financial filings have both — hybrid is the correct default, not an optional extra. |
| **Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)** | Cormack, Clarke, Büttcher, SIGIR 2009, DOI 10.1145/1571941.1572114 (foundational, pre-2026, still canonical — no newer replacement found). | Use as the default fusion method combining BM25 + dense ranked lists; avoids score-scale normalization problems that plague naive linear-combination fusion. |
| **Cross-encoder reranking** | Cohere Rerank v3.5 (`docs.cohere.com/docs/rerank`) — managed, strong finance/semi-structured performance claimed. BGE-reranker-v2-m3 (BAAI, Apache-2.0, self-hostable). Jina Reranker v2/v3 (lowest latency, listwise). Exact latency/accuracy numbers above are from third-party aggregator blogs, **not vendor-published benchmarks — flagged as directional only**. | Add reranking as the second stage after hybrid retrieval when the fused top-k still has noisy ordering — standard two-stage pattern. Self-hostable BGE-reranker is the pragmatic default for a "no vendor API key" bounded reference build. |
| **Parent-child / small-to-big chunking** | LlamaIndex official docs (`developers.llamaindex.ai/.../advanced_retrieval/`) — index small/child chunks for retrieval precision, synthesize from larger parent chunks for context. | Directly applicable: child = sentence/table-row, parent = section/footnote. Preserves units, fiscal-period definitions, and disclaimers that a bare numeric chunk loses. |
| **Contextual Retrieval** | Anthropic engineering blog, `anthropic.com/research/contextual-retrieval` (2024, still canonical, directly fetched/confirmed). Verbatim reported numbers: **49% reduction in failed-retrieval rate** (contextual embeddings + contextual BM25); **67% reduction** when combined with reranking. (A commonly repeated "35% from embeddings alone" figure appears only in secondary cookbook sources, **not verbatim on the blog page fetched — flagged**.) | High-value technique for filings: prepending LLM-generated context ("this chunk is Apple's Q2 2023 10-Q, Item 1, Net Sales table, 3 months ended...") directly addresses the common failure mode of an unretrievable bare number. Requires an LLM call per chunk at indexing time — cost tradeoff, mitigated by prompt caching. |
| **Late interaction (ColBERT family)** | ColBERT (SIGIR 2020), ColBERTv2 (NAACL 2022, `aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.272`) — token-level MaxSim matching, 6-10x storage reduction via residual compression (v2). | Useful where exact phrase/term matching at sub-document granularity matters; higher storage/compute cost than single-vector dense — reserve for a reranking or specialized table-lookup path rather than the primary index in a bounded reference build. |
| **ColPali (vision-based table/layout retrieval)** | ColPali, arXiv:2407.01449, ICLR 2025 (confirmed). Treats each page as an image, late-interaction VLM scoring — avoids OCR/layout loss for tables/charts. Successors noted (ColQwen2.5, ColNomic-3b); one 2026 academic successor ("Argus-Retriever", EMNLP 2026, `aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.352`) reports 92.67 NDCG@5 on ViDoRe V1 — **this is the one genuinely 2026-dated citation found in this research pass**, all other late-interaction citations are pre-2026 foundational work, explicitly noted so as not to overstate novelty. | Directly relevant for SEC filing tables/exhibits with complex layout that text-extraction mangles. Requires a VLM + GPU — explicitly **out of scope for this project's bounded no-GPU/no-keys reference implementation**, documented here as a production upgrade path. |
| **Embedding models** | BGE-M3 (BAAI, arXiv:2402.03216) — single backbone, dense+sparse+multi-vector in one model, multilingual, up to 8192 tokens. Qwen3-Embedding (0.6B/4B/8B, arXiv:2506.05176, `github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Embedding`) — instruction-aware, up to 32K context. Current MTEB standing (2026) per secondary aggregator sources only, **not the live MTEB leaderboard fetched directly — flagged, verify at `huggingface.co/spaces/mteb/leaderboard` before any procurement decision**: NV-Embed-v2 (~72.3), Qwen3-Embedding-8B (~72.05), Voyage-3-large (~70.3, proprietary, common in production), Gemini Embedding 001 (~68.3, multimodal), OpenAI text-embedding-3-large (~64.6). | For the bounded reference build (no GPU, no vendor keys by default): BGE-M3's dense+sparse output in one pass is attractive for self-hosted hybrid search; the remote-provider path can swap in OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large` or an Anthropic-compatible provider via the same interface. Treat exact MTEB rank ordering as perishable — do not hardcode a "best model" claim into code or docs beyond "a measured, swappable choice." |

**Table-specific challenge (grounding citation):** FinQA (EMNLP 2021) and TAT-QA (ACL 2021) both explicitly identify (a) evidence localization across heterogeneous table/text and (b) compositional numerical reasoning as the dominant failure modes for financial-document QA — this is the stated motivation in both papers, not an inferred claim, and directly justifies the table-aware chunking + hybrid retrieval + reranking design chosen below. No benchmark newer than 2021 was found to have superseded FinQA/TAT-QA specifically for this SEC-filing table+text niche as of this research pass — a genuine gap, not an oversight.

| **RAPTOR** | Official repo `parthsarthi03/raptor` (author-confirmed), paper arXiv:2401.18059 (Stanford). | **Corrective RAG (CRAG)** | Methodology consistently described across sources: lightweight retrieval evaluator scores docs Correct/Incorrect/Ambiguous; Correct → knowledge-strip refinement; Incorrect → web-search fallback; Ambiguous → blend. Official repo (`HuskyInSalt/CRAG`) **not independently fetched this session — flagged as plausible but unconfirmed**. | — | Design pattern worth adopting conceptually (retrieval confidence gating + fallback/refusal) even without the full web-search-fallback machinery, which is inappropriate for a closed financial-disclosure corpus (would invite ungrounded external answers). |
| **Self-RAG** | arXiv:2310.11511, project page `selfrag.github.io` — **verified by direct fetch**. Trains the LM to emit reflection tokens (`Retrieve`, `IsRel`, `IsSup`, `IsUse`) for on-demand retrieval and self-assessed groundedness. | Confirmed claim (project page): outperforms ChatGPT/retrieval-augmented Llama2-Chat on open-domain QA/fact verification in the original paper's evaluation. | Requires fine-tuning a dedicated model — out of scope for a bounded, provider-agnostic reference build that must work against off-the-shelf OpenAI/Anthropic endpoints; the *pattern* (explicit relevance/groundedness self-check before answering) is adopted as a prompt-level technique instead (see ARCHITECTURE.md §Agentic loop). |

**Comparative judgment (author-sourced, not this document's own opinion):** graph-based retrieval earns its added cost specifically for multi-hop/cross-document relational queries; hybrid dense+BM25+rerank remains competitive and far cheaper for single-hop fact lookup — which is the dominant query pattern for "what was revenue in Q2" style financial-disclosure questions. **Decision: start with hybrid+rerank+contextual chunking; treat GraphRAG/LightRAG/HippoRAG as a measured, evidence-triggered upgrade (see Architecture §Future Work), not a day-one requirement.**

## 6. Eval protocol and ablation plan (design, not yet executed)

**Eval sets:** FinanceBench open-150 sample (NC-licensed, attribution required) + TAT-QA (CC-BY-4.0, permissive) as primary; FinQA/ConvFinQA for numerical-reasoning regression; FinDER (NC-licensed) for retrieval-precision-only stress testing (short/ambiguous professional queries), not for generation-quality scoring, given its attribution-only terms of use.

**Metrics:**
- Retrieval: Recall@k, nDCG@k, MRR — computed against each dataset's gold evidence spans/chunks.
- Generation: exact-match / numeric-tolerance match for FinQA/TAT-QA-style arithmetic answers; citation-grounding rate (does the cited chunk actually support the claim — checkable algorithmically against gold evidence, not just LLM self-report).
- Closed-book control: run the same questions with retrieval disabled to estimate pretraining-leakage contribution to any "good" score (per §1.3 leakage mitigation).

**Planned ablations** (one variable changed at a time against a fixed query set):
1. BM25-only vs dense-only vs hybrid+RRF — quantifies the hybrid benefit for this corpus specifically (vendor docs confirm the mechanism; the actual % benefit is corpus/query-mix dependent per §2 and must be measured locally, not assumed from the WANDS e-commerce figure cited in secondary sources).
2. Hybrid+RRF vs hybrid+RRF+rerank — quantifies reranker contribution/cost.
3. Flat chunking vs parent-child chunking vs contextual-retrieval-augmented chunking — quantifies Anthropic's reported 49%/67% failure-rate reduction pattern on this specific financial corpus (not assumed to transfer at the same magnitude).
4. Single-collection vs ACL-filtered multi-tenant retrieval — confirms no accuracy regression from Postgres RLS / Qdrant payload-filter scoping.
5. (Future, evidence-gated) hybrid+rerank vs LightRAG/HippoRAG graph layer on a constructed multi-hop/cross-filing query subset only — per §3, graph RAG is expected to help specifically here, not on single-hop queries; this ablation should precede any decision to add a graph layer.
